# Hip Replacement Surgery

## Restore Mobility. Reduce Pain. Improve Quality of Life.

Hip Replacement Surgery, also known as **Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA)**, is a highly successful orthopedic procedure used to relieve chronic hip pain and restore joint function when non-surgical treatments are no longer effective.

The procedure involves replacing damaged parts of the hip joint with artificial implants designed to improve movement, reduce pain, and help patients return to an active lifestyle.

---

## What Is Hip Replacement Surgery?

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint where the head of the femur (thigh bone) fits into the acetabulum (hip socket).

Over time, arthritis, injury, or joint degeneration can damage the cartilage that allows smooth movement. When the cartilage wears away, bones rub against each other, causing pain, stiffness, and limited mobility.

Hip replacement surgery removes the damaged portions of the joint and replaces them with artificial components made from medical-grade metal, ceramic, or durable plastic.

---

## When Is Hip Replacement Recommended?

Your orthopedic surgeon may recommend hip replacement if you experience:

* Persistent hip pain that limits daily activities
* Difficulty walking, climbing stairs, or standing
* Hip stiffness affecting movement
* Pain during rest or sleep
* Reduced quality of life due to hip discomfort
* Failure of medications, physiotherapy, injections, or other conservative treatments

---

## Conditions Treated by Hip Replacement

Hip replacement may be recommended for patients with:

### Osteoarthritis

The most common reason for hip replacement. Cartilage gradually wears away due to age-related degeneration.

### Rheumatoid Arthritis

An autoimmune condition that causes inflammation and damage to the hip joint.

### Avascular Necrosis (AVN)

Loss of blood supply to the hip bone, causing bone collapse and joint damage.

### Hip Fractures

Severe fractures in older adults may require hip replacement for optimal recovery.

### Post-Traumatic Arthritis

Arthritis that develops after injury or trauma to the hip.

### Congenital Hip Disorders

Structural abnormalities that lead to early joint degeneration.

---

## Types of Hip Replacement

### Total Hip Replacement (THR)

Both the ball and socket portions of the hip joint are replaced.

Best for:

* Advanced arthritis
* Severe joint damage
* Long-term pain and disability

### Partial Hip Replacement

Only the damaged femoral head is replaced.

Usually recommended for:

* Certain hip fractures
* Selected elderly patients

### Revision Hip Replacement

Performed when a previous hip replacement requires correction or replacement.

---

## Who Is a Good Candidate?

You may be a suitable candidate if:

* Hip pain interferes with daily life
* Walking distance is significantly reduced
* Conservative treatments no longer provide relief
* Imaging shows severe joint damage
* You are medically fit for surgery
* Your quality of life is significantly affected

Age alone is not the deciding factor. Many patients in their 50s, 60s, 70s, and even older benefit from hip replacement surgery.

---

## Who May Not Be an Ideal Candidate?

Hip replacement may not be immediately recommended for patients with:

* Active infections
* Severe uncontrolled medical conditions
* Significant obesity requiring weight reduction first
* Poor bone quality in certain situations
* Untreated cardiovascular conditions
* Severe neurological disorders affecting mobility

Your orthopedic surgeon will evaluate your overall health before recommending surgery.

## Disadvantages (Cons)

### Surgical Risks

As with any surgery, risks include infection, bleeding, and blood clots.

### Recovery Period

Several weeks to months of rehabilitation are required.

### Implant Wear Over Time

Artificial joints may eventually wear out and require revision surgery.

### Risk of Dislocation

Though uncommon, the artificial joint can dislocate.

### Activity Limitations

Certain high-impact activities may be discouraged after surgery.

### Revision Surgery May Be Needed

Younger patients may require another replacement later in life.

## Preparing for Surgery

Before surgery, patients usually undergo:

* Orthopedic consultation
* Blood investigations
* ECG and medical clearance
* X-rays or advanced imaging
* Medication review
* Pre-operative physiotherapy guidance
* Anesthesia evaluation

Patients will receive instructions regarding fasting, medications, and hospital admission procedures.

---

## What Happens During Surgery?

1. Anesthesia is administered.
2. The surgeon accesses the hip joint.
3. Damaged bone and cartilage are removed.
4. The artificial socket is implanted.
5. The artificial femoral component is inserted.
6. Joint stability and alignment are tested.
7. The incision is closed and dressed.

The procedure typically takes 1 to 2 hours.

---

## Recovery and Rehabilitation

### Hospital Stay

Most patients stay in the hospital for 2–5 days depending on recovery progress.

### Early Mobilization

Patients are encouraged to begin walking with assistance shortly after surgery.

### Physiotherapy

A structured rehabilitation program helps:

* Improve strength
* Restore mobility
* Improve balance
* Prevent stiffness
* Accelerate recovery

### Home Recovery

Patients should:

* Follow prescribed exercises
* Use walking aids when advised
* Attend follow-up appointments
* Maintain a healthy weight
* Follow surgeon recommendations

---

## Possible Risks and Complications

Although uncommon, potential complications include:

* Infection
* Blood clots
* Bleeding
* Nerve injury
* Implant loosening
* Joint dislocation
* Leg length differences
* Fracture around the implant
* Need for revision surgery

Our orthopedic team follows strict safety protocols to minimize these risks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a hip replacement last?

Most modern implants last 15–25 years or longer depending on activity level and overall health.

### When can I walk after surgery?

Many patients begin standing and walking with assistance within 24 hours.

### Will I need physiotherapy?

Yes. Physiotherapy is an essential part of successful recovery.

### Can I return to normal activities?

Most patients return to routine daily activities within several weeks, although complete recovery may take several months.

### Is hip replacement painful?

Pain is managed using modern anesthesia techniques, medications, and rehabilitation programs. Most patients experience significant long-term pain relief.
