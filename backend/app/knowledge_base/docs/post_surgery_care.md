# Post Surgery Care — Navajeevana Ortho Hospitals

## Purpose

This document provides general recovery guidance for patients after orthopedic surgery.

The information in this document is educational and informational only.

Recovery timelines vary depending on:

* Type of surgery
* Age
* Overall health
* Existing medical conditions
* Rehabilitation progress
* Doctor recommendations

Patients must always follow instructions provided by their treating surgeon.

If there is any conflict between this document and a doctor's instructions, the doctor's instructions take priority.

---

# General Recovery Guidelines

## How long is the hospital stay after orthopedic surgery?

| Surgery Type            | Typical Hospital Stay |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| Knee Replacement        | 3–5 days              |
| Hip Replacement         | 3–5 days              |
| Fracture Fixation       | 1–5 days              |
| Arthroscopy             | Same day or 1 day     |
| Ligament Reconstruction | 1–2 days              |

Actual stay duration depends on recovery progress and medical condition.

---

## When can I go home after surgery?

Discharge is generally considered when:

* Pain is reasonably controlled
* Vital signs are stable
* The patient can safely move with assistance if required
* The surgeon determines discharge is appropriate

Final discharge decisions are made by the treating doctor.

---

## What should I arrange at home before discharge?

Patients may benefit from:

* Safe walking space
* Handrails
* Grab bars in bathrooms
* Non-slip flooring
* Adequate lighting
* Assistance from family members
* Walker or crutches if prescribed

---

# Wound Care

## How should I care for my surgical wound?

Patients should:

* Keep the wound clean and dry
* Follow dressing instructions provided by hospital staff
* Wash hands before touching wound dressings
* Avoid applying creams, powders, or home remedies unless advised by a doctor

---

## When should the dressing be changed?

Dressing schedules vary depending on:

* Type of surgery
* Wound condition
* Doctor instructions

Patients should follow the discharge instructions provided by the surgical team.

---

## Can I take a shower after surgery?

This depends on:

* Type of surgery
* Wound healing status
* Dressing type

Patients should follow the instructions provided by their surgeon.

---

## When are stitches or staples removed?

In many cases, stitches or staples are removed approximately 10–14 days after surgery.

The exact timing is determined by the surgeon.

---

# Warning Signs of Wound Problems

Contact the hospital immediately if you notice:

* Increasing redness
* Increasing swelling
* Warmth around the wound
* Pus or discharge
* Foul smell from wound
* Wound opening
* Persistent bleeding
* Fever

These symptoms require medical review.

---

# Pain Management

## Is pain normal after surgery?

Yes.

Some pain and discomfort are expected during recovery.

Pain usually improves gradually as healing progresses.

---

## What pain medicines will be prescribed?

Medication decisions are made only by the treating doctor.

The AI system does not provide medication recommendations, dosage instructions, or treatment advice.

---

## How long does post-surgical pain last?

Recovery experiences vary.

Some discomfort may continue for several weeks or months depending on:

* Surgery type
* Healing progress
* Rehabilitation activity

---

## When should I contact the hospital about pain?

Contact the hospital if:

* Pain suddenly worsens
* Pain becomes severe
* Pain is associated with swelling
* Pain is associated with fever
* Pain prevents normal recovery activities

---

# Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation

## When does physiotherapy start?

Many orthopedic patients begin rehabilitation shortly after surgery.

The exact schedule depends on:

* Surgery type
* Surgeon recommendations
* Patient condition

---

## Why is physiotherapy important?

Physiotherapy helps improve:

* Mobility
* Strength
* Joint movement
* Functional recovery

---

## Can physiotherapy be done at home?

In some situations, home-based physiotherapy may be appropriate.

Patients should follow guidance from their physiotherapy team.

---

## How long does physiotherapy continue?

Recovery programs vary.

Some patients require only a few weeks while others may require several months of rehabilitation.

---

# Mobility and Activity

## When can I start walking after surgery?

Walking timelines vary by procedure.

Many patients begin supervised walking early in recovery.

Patients should follow surgeon and physiotherapy instructions.

---

## Do I need a walker or crutches?

Some patients may require:

* Walker
* Crutches
* Walking stick

The decision depends on the procedure and recovery status.

---

## When can I climb stairs?

Stair climbing is usually introduced gradually when approved by the healthcare team.

---

## When can I drive?

Driving should only resume when:

* Safe movement is possible
* Pain is adequately controlled
* The surgeon approves

---

## When can I return to work?

| Surgery Type     | Desk Work | Physically Demanding Work |
| ---------------- | --------- | ------------------------- |

| Hip Replacement  | 4–8 weeks | 8–12 weeks or longer      |
| Fracture Surgery | Varies    | Varies                    |

Actual timelines depend on recovery and surgeon recommendations.

# Diet After Surgery

## What should I eat after orthopedic surgery?

A balanced recovery diet may include:

* Protein-rich foods
* Fruits
* Vegetables
* Whole grains
* Calcium-rich foods
* Vitamin D sources
* Adequate fluids

Good nutrition supports healing. ([nhs.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-guidelines-and-food-labels/the-eatwell-guide/))

---

## What foods should be limited?

Patients should limit:

* Excessively processed foods
* Excess sugar
* Excess alcohol

unless otherwise advised by their doctor.

---

## Do I need calcium or vitamin supplements?

Supplement decisions should be made by the treating doctor.

The AI system does not recommend supplements.

# Follow-Up Visits

## When is the first follow-up appointment?

Most patients receive follow-up instructions before discharge.

The timing depends on:

* Surgery type
* Recovery progress
* Surgeon recommendations

---

## What happens during follow-up appointments?

The doctor may:

* Review recovery progress
* Assess wound healing
* Evaluate mobility
* Review rehabilitation progress
* Discuss ongoing recovery goals

---

## Will I need repeat X-rays?

Some patients may require follow-up imaging.

The decision is made by the treating surgeon.

---

# Warning Signs — Seek Immediate Medical Attention

Patients should seek immediate medical attention if they experience:

* Chest pain
* Difficulty breathing
* Severe shortness of breath
* Sudden severe swelling
* Severe bleeding
* High fever
* Loss of consciousness
* Sudden inability to move the operated limb
* Severe trauma after surgery
* Fall with suspected injury

These symptoms may require urgent medical evaluation.

## Will my implant trigger airport metal detectors?

Some orthopedic implants may be detected by security systems.

Patients may inform security personnel about their implant history if required.

---

## Does the implant need to be removed later?

Many orthopedic implants remain in place permanently.

Removal decisions are made by the treating orthopedic surgeon.

---

## How long do joint replacement implants last?

Implant lifespan varies depending on:

* Implant type
* Activity level
* Overall health
* Surgical factors

Patients should attend regular follow-up appointments as recommended.

---

# Frequently Asked Recovery Questions

## Is swelling normal after surgery?

Mild swelling may occur during recovery.

Sudden or worsening swelling should be reported to the hospital.

---

## Is bruising normal after surgery?

Some bruising can occur after orthopedic procedures.

Patients should contact the hospital if bruising is extensive or worsening significantly.

---

## Can I travel after surgery?

Travel plans should be discussed with the treating surgeon.

Travel timing depends on recovery progress and medical condition.

---

## When should I contact the hospital?

Contact the hospital whenever:

* Recovery is not progressing as expected
* New symptoms appear
* Wound concerns develop
* Mobility suddenly worsens
* You are uncertain about post-operative instructions

When in doubt, contact the hospital or seek medical attention.
