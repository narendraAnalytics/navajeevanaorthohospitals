# Robotic Joint Replacement

## Advanced Precision. Personalized Surgery. Better Recovery.

Robotic Joint Replacement is one of the most advanced developments in modern orthopedic surgery. It combines the expertise of highly skilled orthopedic surgeons with robotic-assisted technology to perform knee and hip replacement procedures with exceptional precision and accuracy.

Contrary to common belief, the robot does not perform the surgery independently. The orthopedic surgeon remains in complete control throughout the procedure while the robotic system provides real-time guidance, advanced planning, and highly accurate implant positioning.

The goal is to improve surgical precision, enhance implant alignment, preserve healthy tissue, and support faster recovery.

---

# What Is Robotic Joint Replacement?

Robotic Joint Replacement is an advanced surgical technique that uses:

* 3D imaging
* Computer-assisted planning
* Real-time surgical guidance
* Robotic-arm assistance

to help surgeons perform joint replacement procedures more accurately.

The robotic system creates a personalized surgical plan based on the patient's anatomy before surgery begins.

During the procedure, the system provides real-time feedback to assist the surgeon in making highly precise bone preparation and implant placement decisions.

---

# Is the Robot Performing the Surgery?

## No.

A common misconception is that the robot performs the surgery independently.

In reality:

* The surgeon performs the operation.
* The surgeon controls every step.
* The robotic system acts as a highly advanced guidance tool.
* The surgeon can adjust decisions at any time.

Think of the robotic system as an advanced navigation system that helps the surgeon achieve greater precision.

# Conditions Commonly Treated

Robotic joint replacement is commonly used for:

## Knee Osteoarthritis

Advanced arthritis causing severe pain and stiffness.

## Hip Osteoarthritis

Joint degeneration affecting mobility and quality of life.

## Rheumatoid Arthritis

Inflammatory arthritis causing joint destruction.

## Avascular Necrosis (AVN)

Loss of blood supply to the hip joint.

## Post-Traumatic Arthritis

Arthritis developing after injury or fracture.

## Severe Joint Deformities

Complex cases requiring highly accurate implant positioning.

# How Robotic Joint Replacement Works

## Step 1: Preoperative Planning

Detailed imaging is used to create a personalized 3D model of the patient's joint.

The surgeon can:

* Analyze anatomy
* Plan implant placement
* Determine alignment
* Optimize surgical strategy

before entering the operating room.

---

## Step 2: Personalized Surgical Plan

Every patient is unique.

The robotic platform helps create a customized surgical plan based on:

* Bone structure
* Joint alignment
* Anatomy
* Implant sizing

---

## Step 3: Robotic-Assisted Surgery

During surgery:

* The surgeon guides the robotic system.
* Real-time feedback improves precision.
* Bone preparation remains within planned boundaries.
* Implant positioning is continuously monitored.

This helps improve accuracy and consistency.

# Who Is a Good Candidate?

You may be a suitable candidate if:

* You have severe knee arthritis
* You have advanced hip arthritis
* Joint pain affects daily life
* Conservative treatment has failed
* You are medically fit for surgery
* Imaging confirms significant joint damage

---

# Who May Not Be a Candidate?

Robotic joint replacement may not be appropriate for:

* Active infections
* Certain severe medical conditions
* Patients who are not medically fit for surgery
* Individuals whose condition can still be managed conservatively

A complete orthopedic evaluation is required before making a decision.
