# Test Preparation — Navajeevana Ortho Hospitals

## Purpose

This document provides general guidance for patients preparing for diagnostic tests and imaging procedures.

The information is intended for educational and scheduling purposes only.

Patients must always follow the instructions provided by:

* Treating doctor
* Radiology department
* Laboratory staff
* Hospital diagnostic team

If instructions from hospital staff differ from this document, the hospital instructions take priority.

---

# General Preparation Guidelines

## Why is test preparation important?

Proper preparation helps:

* Improve test accuracy
* Reduce delays
* Avoid repeat testing
* Improve patient safety
* Improve diagnostic quality

---

## Should I follow instructions given by my doctor?

Yes.

Patients should always follow instructions provided by their treating doctor and diagnostic team.

---

# General Fasting Rules

## Do I need to fast before a blood test?

Some blood tests require fasting while others do not.

Fasting requirements depend on the specific test ordered.

Patients should verify fasting requirements during appointment scheduling.

---

## What does fasting mean?

Fasting generally means:

* No food
* No snacks
* No sugary beverages

Water is usually allowed unless instructed otherwise.

---

## Can I drink water while fasting?

In many cases, plain water is permitted.

Patients should follow instructions provided by the laboratory.

---

## Can I drink tea, coffee, or juice while fasting?

No.

Tea, coffee, milk, soft drinks, juice, and flavored beverages may affect certain test results.

---

## Do I need to fast before an X-ray?

Most routine X-rays do not require fasting.

Specific instructions may be provided for specialized procedures.

---

## Do I need to fast before an MRI?

Many MRI examinations do not require fasting.

Some MRI studies may require special preparation.

Patients should follow instructions provided by the radiology department.

---

## Do I need to fast before a CT scan?

Some CT scans may require fasting, particularly when contrast material is planned.

Preparation requirements vary depending on the examination.

---

# MRI Preparation

## What is an MRI scan?

MRI uses a strong magnetic field and radio waves to create detailed images of the body. ([radiologyinfo.org](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/safety-mr))

---

## What should I do before an MRI?

Patients may be asked to:

* Arrive early
* Complete safety screening forms
* Remove metallic objects
* Provide information about implants and previous surgeries

---

## What items are not allowed in the MRI room?

Examples include:

* Jewelry
* Watches
* Hairpins
* Hearing aids
* Credit cards
* Mobile phones
* Keys
* Metallic accessories

MRI safety screening is important because MRI machines use powerful magnets. ([radiologyinfo.org](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/safety-mr))

---

## Should I tell the staff about implants?

Yes.

Patients should inform staff about:

* Joint replacements
* Surgical implants
* Metal plates
* Metal screws
* Pacemakers
* Cochlear implants
* Previous surgeries

---

## How long does an MRI scan take?

Many MRI examinations take approximately 20–60 minutes.

Actual duration depends on the body part being examined and the study type.

---

## Will I receive contrast dye?

Some MRI examinations require contrast.

The radiologist and treating doctor determine whether contrast is necessary.

---

## What if I am claustrophobic?

Patients should inform staff before the examination.

Alternative arrangements may be available depending on the situation.

---

# CT Scan Preparation

## What is a CT scan?

CT scanning uses X-rays and computer technology to create detailed cross-sectional images of the body. ([radiologyinfo.org](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/safety-ct))

---

## How should I prepare for a CT scan?

Preparation depends on:

* Body part being examined
* Whether contrast is used
* Clinical indication

Patients should follow instructions provided by the radiology department.

---

## Is contrast dye used during CT scans?

Some CT studies use contrast material.

The need for contrast depends on the examination.

---

## Are there side effects from contrast?

Most patients tolerate contrast material well.

Patients should inform staff about:

* Previous contrast reactions
* Kidney disease
* Allergies

---

## How long does a CT scan take?

Many CT examinations are completed within 10–30 minutes.

---

# X-Ray Preparation

## What is an X-ray?

An X-ray is a commonly used imaging examination that helps doctors evaluate bones, joints, fractures, and other structures.

---

## Do I need special preparation for an X-ray?

Most routine orthopedic X-rays require little or no preparation.

---

## How long does an X-ray take?

Many orthopedic X-rays are completed within a few minutes.

---

## Is X-ray safe during pregnancy?

Patients who are pregnant or may be pregnant should inform the radiology team before imaging.

Pregnancy-related imaging questions require review by medical staff.

# Arriving for Your Test

## How early should I arrive?

Patients should arrive approximately 15–30 minutes before the scheduled appointment.

---

## What documents should I bring?

Patients may be asked to bring:

* Doctor referral or prescription
* Previous reports
* Aadhaar Card
* Insurance card
* Aarogyasri card
* PM-JAY card
* CGHS card (if applicable)

---

## What should I wear?

Comfortable clothing is recommended.

Patients may be asked to remove metallic items during imaging procedures.
