# Spine Surgery

## Relieve Pain. Restore Function. Return to Life.

Spine surgery is performed to treat conditions affecting the neck, upper back, lower back, spinal nerves, and spinal cord when conservative treatments such as medications, physiotherapy, injections, and lifestyle modifications have not provided sufficient relief.

Modern spine surgery techniques, including minimally invasive procedures, allow many patients to recover faster with less pain and smaller incisions.

---

# Understanding the Spine

The spine consists of:

* Cervical Spine (Neck)
* Thoracic Spine (Upper Back)
* Lumbar Spine (Lower Back)
* Sacrum and Coccyx

The spine supports the body, protects the spinal cord, and enables movement and flexibility.

Damage, degeneration, injury, or nerve compression can cause significant pain and disability.

---

# When Is Spine Surgery Recommended?

Spine surgery may be recommended when:

* Severe back or neck pain persists
* Numbness or weakness develops in the arms or legs
* Walking becomes difficult
* Nerve compression causes symptoms
* Conservative treatments fail
* Spinal instability is present
* Progressive neurological deficits occur
* Quality of life is significantly affected

---

# Conditions Treated by Spine Surgery

## Herniated Disc (Slipped Disc)

Occurs when a spinal disc presses on nearby nerves, causing pain, numbness, or weakness.

## Spinal Stenosis

Narrowing of the spinal canal that places pressure on nerves.

## Degenerative Disc Disease

Age-related deterioration of spinal discs causing chronic pain.

## Sciatica

Compression of the sciatic nerve causing pain that radiates into the leg.

## Scoliosis

Abnormal curvature of the spine.

## Spinal Fractures

Fractures resulting from trauma or osteoporosis.

## Spondylolisthesis

A condition where one vertebra slips over another.

## Spinal Tumors

Growths affecting the spine or spinal cord.

---

# Types of Spine Surgery

## Microdiscectomy

Removal of a portion of a herniated disc that is compressing a nerve.

Best for:

* Sciatica
* Herniated discs
* Nerve compression

---

## Laminectomy

Removal of part of the vertebral bone to relieve pressure on nerves.

Best for:

* Spinal stenosis
* Nerve compression

---

## Spinal Fusion

Two or more vertebrae are permanently joined together to improve stability.

Best for:

* Spinal instability
* Spondylolisthesis
* Severe degenerative conditions

---

## Artificial Disc Replacement

Damaged discs are replaced with artificial implants while preserving motion.

Best for:

* Selected degenerative disc disease patients

---

## Minimally Invasive Spine Surgery

Performed through small incisions with less muscle damage and faster recovery.

Benefits include:

* Smaller scars
* Reduced blood loss
* Shorter hospital stay
* Faster recovery

# Who Is a Good Candidate?

You may be a candidate if:

* Symptoms significantly affect daily life
* Walking ability is reduced
* Conservative treatments have failed
* Imaging confirms spinal pathology
* You are medically fit for surgery
* Neurological symptoms are worsening

---

# Who May Not Be a Candidate?

Surgery may not be immediately recommended if:

* Symptoms are mild
* Conservative treatment has not been attempted
* Active infection is present
* Certain severe medical conditions are uncontrolled
* Surgical risks outweigh benefits

Your spine specialist will determine the most appropriate treatment plan.

# Preparing for Surgery

Before surgery, patients may require:

* Orthopedic or spine specialist consultation
* Blood tests
* ECG and medical clearance
* MRI or CT scan
* Medication review
* Anesthesia assessment
* Physiotherapy consultation

---

# What Happens During Surgery?

1. Anesthesia is administered.
2. The surgeon accesses the affected area.
3. The underlying spinal problem is treated.
4. Nerves are decompressed if necessary.
5. Stabilization devices may be inserted if required.
6. The incision is closed and dressed.

Procedure duration varies depending on the complexity of surgery.

---

# Recovery After Spine Surgery

Recovery varies according to the type of procedure performed.

Most patients begin walking shortly after surgery under medical supervision. Early walking is often encouraged as part of recovery.

---

# What To Do After Spine Surgery

## Follow Your Surgeon's Instructions

Always follow the specific instructions provided by your healthcare team.

## Walk Regularly

Walking improves circulation, prevents blood clots, and promotes healing.

Start with short walks and gradually increase distance as advised.

## Maintain Good Posture

Keep your spine aligned while sitting, standing, and walking. Avoid slouching.

## Attend Physiotherapy

Physiotherapy helps:

* Improve strength
* Increase flexibility
* Restore mobility
* Prevent stiffness
* Improve balance

## Eat a Healthy Diet

Focus on:

* Protein-rich foods
* Fruits and vegetables
* Calcium-rich foods
* Vitamin D sources
* Adequate hydration

Good nutrition supports healing.

## Care for the Surgical Wound

* Keep the incision clean and dry
* Follow dressing instructions
* Watch for signs of infection

---

# What NOT To Do After Spine Surgery

## Do Not Lift Heavy Objects

Avoid lifting heavy weights until cleared by your surgeon.

## Avoid Bending and Twisting

Sudden bending or twisting can stress the healing spine.

## Avoid Prolonged Sitting

Long periods of sitting may place excessive pressure on the spine. Change positions regularly.

## Do Not Smoke

Smoking can impair healing and increase complications.

## Avoid Alcohol During Early Recovery

Alcohol may interfere with medications and recovery.

## Avoid High-Impact Activities

Do not:

* Run
* Jump
* Play contact sports
* Lift weights

until approved by your surgeon.

---

# Warning Signs - Contact Your Doctor Immediately

Seek medical attention if you experience:

* Fever
* Increasing pain
* Redness around the incision
* Drainage from the wound
* New weakness
* Loss of bladder control
* Loss of bowel control
* Severe numbness

These symptoms require prompt medical evaluation.

Yes. Physiotherapy is often an important part of recovery.

## Can spine surgery completely eliminate pain?

Many patients experience significant pain relief, but outcomes vary based on the condition being treated.

## When can I drive?

Driving should only be resumed after approval from your surgeon.

## Will I be able to exercise again?

Most patients gradually return to exercise after healing and rehabilitation.
