# What Is a Fracture?

A fracture is a break, crack, or disruption in the continuity of a bone.

Fractures can range from minor hairline cracks to severe injuries involving multiple broken bone fragments and damage to surrounding tissues.

Some fractures heal with non-surgical treatment, while others require surgery to restore proper alignment and function.

---

# What Is Orthopedic Trauma?

Orthopedic trauma refers to injuries affecting:

* Bones
* Joints
* Muscles
* Ligaments
* Tendons
* Nerves
* Soft tissues

These injuries commonly occur due to:

* Road traffic accidents
* Falls
* Sports injuries
* Workplace accidents
* Industrial injuries
* Crush injuries
* High-impact trauma

# Types of Fractures

## Closed Fracture

The bone is broken but does not penetrate the skin.

---

## Open (Compound) Fracture

The broken bone communicates with the external environment through a wound. Open fractures are orthopedic emergencies because of the risk of infection and often require urgent surgery, antibiotics, wound cleaning, and stabilization.

---

## Displaced Fracture

Bone fragments have moved out of their normal alignment.

---

## Non-Displaced Fracture

The bone remains in proper alignment despite the break.

---

## Comminuted Fracture

The bone breaks into multiple pieces.

---

## Stress Fracture

Small cracks caused by repetitive stress and overuse.

---

## Compression Fracture

Commonly affects the spine and may occur due to osteoporosis.

---

# Signs and Symptoms of a Fracture

Seek medical attention if you experience:

* Severe pain
* Swelling
* Bruising
* Visible deformity
* Difficulty moving the limb
* Inability to bear weight
* Tenderness
* Numbness or tingling
* Open wounds near the injury

---

# Emergency First Aid for Suspected Fractures

## What To Do

### Keep the Injured Area Still

Avoid unnecessary movement.

### Apply Ice

Ice may help reduce swelling and pain.

### Elevate the Limb

If possible, elevate the injured area.

### Seek Medical Care Immediately

Prompt treatment improves outcomes.

### Call Emergency Services for Serious Injuries

Especially if:

* There is severe bleeding
* The bone is visible
* The patient cannot move
* There are multiple injuries

---

## What NOT To Do

### Do Not Attempt to Realign the Bone

Improper handling may worsen the injury.

### Do Not Force Movement

This may damage nerves, blood vessels, or soft tissues.

### Do Not Ignore Severe Pain

Persistent pain requires medical evaluation.

### Do Not Delay Treatment

Early treatment reduces complications.

---

# Non-Surgical Treatment

Many fractures heal without surgery.

## Casting

A cast immobilizes the bone while it heals.

## Splinting

Often used during the early stage of treatment.

## Bracing

Provides support while allowing limited movement.

## Activity Modification

Temporary restrictions help protect the healing bone.

Immobilization is a key part of fracture treatment and helps maintain proper alignment during healing.

---

# Surgical Treatment

Surgery may be recommended for:

* Displaced fractures
* Open fractures
* Unstable fractures
* Multiple fractures
* Joint fractures

---

## Internal Fixation

The surgeon uses:

* Plates
* Screws
* Rods
* Nails

to stabilize the fracture while healing occurs.

---

## External Fixation

A stabilizing frame is placed outside the body and connected to the bone through pins.

Often used for:

* Severe trauma
* Open fractures
* Complex injuries

---

## Open Reduction and Internal Fixation (ORIF)

A surgical procedure used to realign and stabilize broken bones using metal implants.

Commonly performed for complex fractures.

---

# Recovery and Rehabilitation

Recovery varies depending on:

* Type of fracture
* Patient age
* Overall health
* Treatment method

Bone healing is a natural process that may take several weeks to months depending on the injury.

---

# Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation plays a crucial role in recovery.

Goals include:

* Restoring movement
* Improving strength
* Preventing stiffness
* Improving balance
* Restoring independence

Physiotherapy often includes:

* Range-of-motion exercises
* Strength training
* Gait training
* Functional rehabilitation

---

# What To Do During Recovery

## Follow Medical Instructions

Attend all scheduled follow-up visits.

## Take Medications as Prescribed

Follow your treatment plan carefully.

## Perform Recommended Exercises

Continue physiotherapy and home exercise programs.

## Eat a Healthy Diet

Focus on:

* Protein-rich foods
* Calcium-rich foods
* Vitamin D
* Fruits and vegetables

## Stay Hydrated

Proper hydration supports healing.

## Use Walking Aids When Needed

Use crutches, walkers, or supports as instructed.

---

# What NOT To Do During Recovery

## Do Not Remove Casts or Braces Yourself

Only a healthcare professional should remove them.

## Avoid Heavy Lifting

Until approved by your orthopedic surgeon.

## Do Not Ignore Pain or Swelling

Persistent symptoms should be evaluated.

## Avoid Smoking

Smoking may delay bone healing.

## Do Not Return to Sports Too Early

Premature activity increases the risk of re-injury.

---

# Possible Complications

Although many fractures heal successfully, complications may occur:

* Infection
* Delayed healing
* Nonunion (failure to heal)
* Malunion (healing in an incorrect position)
* Joint stiffness
* Chronic pain
* Nerve injury
* Blood clots

Prompt treatment and regular follow-up help reduce these risks.
